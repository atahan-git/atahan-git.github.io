Another Friday, and time for the next **Games I've Played Recently**. Occasionally it's a lot of questioning to make these lists, because what games do I include? Sometimes I play games a little bit <2h, and drop it. Should I include those? Some full games are shorter than that! How about games that are maybe not-entirely appropriate to share in a work context? Not all games fit neatly into "acceptable" and "not-acceptable" after all. How about never ending games like Helldivers 2 that I did play a bunch in these past months, but like, it feels weird to keep including them as I never quite stopped and came back to it. Oh well, enough with the meta-commentary, here's the **Part 2**:

- **Idle Deepcore:** Yet another entry in my ever growing list of idle games. In Idle Deepcore you buy a bunch of machines to dig down on plots of land you also buy. This game has the bit about idle games I like the most: You have a bunch of levers to pull to optimize your progress, then you look at other things while resources are produced, and now you buy some new machines that present a slightly different optimization problem. And this one has the great "operational randomness". Different machines interact differently when digging, so the best strategy isn't always buy as many of the best machines. This game even has a super late game "automate machine and land buying" stage, but I got bored by that time. When I was playing it, it did lack the other thing I like from idle incremental games, an end goal, but between when I played it and when I'm writing this they added one! Maybe I'll return someday to try to get to that win state. Oh well.
- **Subnautica 2 (early access):** This game had some drama in the making that I was following as I love the original Subnautica, but even with all the drama the released product is pretty good! In Subnautica 2 you find yourself on a planet covered by an ocean. This time there is an ominous massive "tree" in the distance that is causing all sorts of problems, including seemingly killing all the other investigation teams sent here before you. Just like in Subnautica 1, in 2 you build a base, gather resources, build yourself underwater vehicles, get scared as the local fauna attacks you (or worse, make scary noises just outside of the range of your lamp), and uncover some mystery about the ship AI and the big alien tree in the distance. Honestly I think Subnautica 1 was better, but Subnautica 2 is a LOT more streamlined. It keeps the best parts and smooths out a lot of the edges from the first game. But I partly loved the first game because of the edges, so your mileage may vary. But for first time players it's a lot easier to get into Subnautica 2 probably. It's still in early access, sooooo it's missing an end and it doesn't have the beloved Cyclops yet (but apparently they are planning on adding a similar vehicle this time!), but even in early access there is a lot of game to enjoy here. I'll be playing this one again once it's fully released.
- **Librarian: Tidy Up the Arcane Library!:** Tidy Up the Arcane Library!: In Librarian you organize an arcane library. As you do that, you unlock spells to help you in your endeavor. I saw this one here in [internal work chat] first, and I didn't play it because I'm not a big fan of organizing games, but surprisingly this one works more like an incremental game. You start by looking at books one by one, but soon you are magically pulling all the missing books to your hand and automatically putting them on shelves. But I enjoyed the first part of the game a LOT as well. The way to make unlocking new powers feel good is to first make the player slog through the process without the magic powers. It was a lot of fun to manually find all the white looking books, learning the patterns, finding the last 2 books for a particular shelf etc. Then it was AMAZING to use spells to find the one final book for a shelf I've been trying to find for 10 minutes and finish the row. Then it was fun optimizing the spell cast order and slowly making them better. Then it was a slog after everything is unlocked and I figured out the optimal spell order and it was just doing that over and over until all the rows were done. This is a game that showed me a glimpse for a new mini genre. Like, they could make an even bigger library, make the skill tree more complex with more gradual unlocks (2 of the spells make the other 2 spells obsolete), maybe add some new challenges to some other rooms (I dunno, like a wrecking ball that makes you drop all your books), get other items to organize that have other carrying challenges, and you have a whole another game! A surprise hit for me for sure.
- **Cosmodrill:** Yet another incremental game. In Cosmodrill, you, an employee that collects materials from asteroids using your drill ship, get sucked into a black hole, a bunch of bad stuff happens, now you need to solve a mystery while upgrading your ship and repairing stations. I finished the whole game in just under 2 hours, but it was a lot of fun. This one has that aspect of balancing which upgrade to buy first, combined with secondary abilities, and a couple of biomes that make it an optimization puzzle to figure out which combination progresses the fastest. And it had an interesting enough story to go with, even though the ending to the story was a bit eh. The digging and buying upgrades were fun the whole way through though, and it was fun to piece together the story to try to predict the final reveal. A fun evening or two for sure.

And that's it for Part 2! I actually cut a bunch of games out of this one, sooo there is only one more part left, but oh well. Have a fun weekend everyone!
