The reverse chronological ordered Games I've Played Recently continues. Now we've reached last summer. Anyways Part 3:

- **The NOexistenceN of you AND me:** This is a very short visual novel that still looks into themes like: Are characters in media alive? Does it matter? What even is a character in a multiple choice story if they change to fit your decisions? This game also works as an ad to their newly released much bigger in scope game, but it has merit on its own. Overall this was short and nice, giving me new ways to look at games/visual novels, and it has striking art. There are also a lot of references to popular internet memes, so I enjoyed how they were handled.
- **Wheel World:** I played this as part of looking at references for gamification at work. In Wheel World you ride around on your bike and do races and learn about some NPCs. That's really about it. The controls were nice, but after you do 2 races there isn't much. Also its artstyle makes it harder to see the terrain which is unfortunate because it is a nice artstyle. It's one of those games that lets you see the potential, but the game never really reaches it. Oh well.
- **The Count of Monte Clicker:** Another very weird clicker. This one mixes a "shoot at incoming enemies" minigame with incremental upgrades and a very intriguing story of how the Count of Monte ended up in a prison. This one was good early on, but later it became too grindy for my taste so I didn't finish it. Still it innovates on the clicker genre and I always like seeing innovations in that genre.
- **Astronomics:** Another grindy game. In this one you go to asteroids with your ship and mine for resources using various robots and tools. I like how you need to balance how much you spend on local infrastructure to maximize your profits. It's also interesting that the resources you mine actively block you from reaching other areas. There is some light combat elements too. This was also a game that was great early on when unlocking new powerful tools happened quickly, but then it gets boring when you do the same mining mission with the same exact tools for too long. There didn't seem to be any other exciting new tools in the research tree later on either, so I also dropped this one without finishing. But also games like this aren't really meant to end, you are supposed to just play them while listening to a podcast or something, which I also did a lot. But again this game is in early access, and I see they are still adding new and fun tools as I checked while writing this, so maybe all those will get fixed eventually.
- **The Alters:** This was the big game of this bunch. In The Alters you are stuck on an alien planet after your ship fails. You control Jan Dolski, and the main gameplay is between going out and collecting resources outside while building infrastructure then coming back home to craft things, expand your base, and talk with your Alters. Through the game you make a handful of clones of yourself, based on a supercomputer that can simulate Jan's full life if he made a different choice earlier in his life. Eg if back in his teens he studied a bit harder and got to college, he becomes Jan Scientist. Both the gameplay and the story were superb in this one. This is also done by the same studio that did Frostpunk and it shows in the care they put into the whole thing. Over your journey you make friends (or enemies) with other Jans, discover the weirdness happening on this planet, and come to terms with Jan's regrets about his various life choices. And the base building is amazing too, I loved figuring out the optimal path to win at the highest difficulty. I love the ending I got too, making peace with the various Jans and giving the original Jan a good life afterwards. The visuals in this game are outstanding too, they rendered the alien planet with amazing details that are both actual science and sci-fi. I paused at various points to just take in the views. But they also don't shy away from making something clearer for gameplay purposes even if it makes no scientific sense lol.

And that's about it for this part. It's a lot of fun to look back and think "oh yeah that game was great!".
