Wow work has been crazy for me recently so this part waited almost a month instead of my usual gap of 1 week. But I think I can write this down now. Behold, Games I've Played Recently part 2!

- **Wall World 2:** I played the original Wall World, so I've been looking forward to the release of this one. And unfortunately, this one isn't as good as the previous one <img class="slack-emoji" src="/images/slack-sadcat-9C027B8052FF.png" alt=":sadcat:" />. In Wall World (1 and 2), you are on a spider robot crawling along a mysterious wall. You stop at cracks in the wall and use your pilot to dig loot. Then you upgrade your spider. And every couple of minutes you need to return back to your spider bot to fight enemies attacking you. The first game had a pretty good loop. Go out, collect money, die quickly, upgrade your gear, now you can go further along the wall and get more money and live (and hence play) longer. In the second one they tried to do this whole different map locations thing, and each location is small, and you do these missions, which means you are often done before dying and have to decide when to call it a day. That ruined the "let's survive another wave!" thing because you almost never die in the second game, and have to just return even if you get an amazing set of powerups in your run. And the upgrades you get feel less useful because half the time the mission is too easy so it doesn't matter, and other times the mission is too hard so you need to grind away in the easy levels. One thing that makes roguelikes great is that -every- part of a run is often important, because you need to scale your power quickly to survive the later levels. That drive goes away when there is a clear mission end point that is too easy to achieve. And having all these different maps also ruined the sense of exploration in the first game that I loved. You needed the upgrades to venture further along the wall and discover all the new biomes. Now you just drop to the new biome after you clear a bunch of boring quests. Oh well, game design lessons learned. Sometimes you need to play something that doesn't work to find out what works.
- **Blue Prince:** I really wanted to like this one. In Blue Prince you are tasked with entering a room in a mansion. But every time you open a door you pick which room is there. The main point is that you are solving these puzzles. I didn't like it because the puzzles were more about "read the text in room X and enter it in room Y" than learning like "room X needs to be next to room Y to make magic happen". There are puzzles of the latter type too, but some of them are so RNG based (you will only ever get room X next to room Y and also connect them to room Z after activating room X and Y with other lesser rooms) that it was annoying. I love roguelikes where you gradually learn tricks like the ones in Blue Prince, but it felt to me I hit a wall at some point where in any run I'm learning absolutely nothing new and I need crazy RNG just to get to the next step. And why did they make the outside room that affects the runs take soooo looong to walk to? This game baffles me. I'm glad they didn't have any RNG in Outer Wilds. Oh well.
- **Dispatch:** This was a surprising gem? In Dispatch you play as a hero who lost his mech so now he needs to dispatch the Z team of heroes made from former villains. And your choices affect the story in interesting ways. This is one of those games that doesn't really do anything new, but does everything it does so well that its just great. The story was fun. Writing was good. The crude humor was great. The hero dispatch minigame was amazingly done, I could play that for hours if they spin it off as its own game. And the art is phenomenal too. If any of those interests you, this is an easy recommendation to play.
- **American Arcadia:** I played this game after watching a video about game endings, specifically how some games just have a pick from 3 endings at the end that sorta defeats the point of everything leading up to that point. In American Arcadia, you are stuck in a "Truman Show" city. Some people living here are actors, some are real people. You are one of the least watched out of all, so stuff happens to you. You run around and don't really make any choices in the game until the end. This game was enjoyable, but nothing too amazing, until the very end where the ending made it sooo much better. I'm not going to spoil it, but the ending put everything in such an interesting perspective. With games like this, the ending usually makes the entire experience retroactively worse, but this one made it better. It made me think. It's a short-ish game (my run was 7h) so I would recommend giving it a go if you want something easy to play but also philosophical. But I do also think they could've cut like 30-50% of the gameplay and still have the same story impact. Oh well. You can just turn your brain off and chill at some of the parts.
