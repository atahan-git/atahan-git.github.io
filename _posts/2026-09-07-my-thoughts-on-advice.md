Advice, Wisdom, Story. 
They are all the same.

In the 10+ years of me making games and learning how to live a life, I have seen many of those. Over time I started noticing things that don't make sense:
1. Same advice is the best thing ever one year, and absolute garbage the next.
2. For any advice, there is the oposite advice.
3. Same advice is boring and unapplicable one year, and interesting and unexpected the next.

So, what's going on with that? I have some theories. 

# Following advice will make you more like the person giving it.
This is the first and the most important point. Advice is just a life lesson someone learned as they came across a problem in their own life. They've done the work, faced some problem, tried many different things, suffered. The solution that worked for them is the advice. 

Choose where you take advice from carefully. The more you follow someone's advice, the more you will end up like them. There is a famous wisdom out there that asserts "You are the average of the 5 people you are most close to". Maybe it's true because those people's advice hold the most weight for you. Whenever they see you facing a problem shaped the same way as they are, they will give you what worked for them. Do it, and you will become more like them. Do it and you will face more of the same problems they face, as well as the opportunities and achievements that brings.

# Most advice only makes sense after you've done "the work" and have the context.
This somewhat follows from the point that there is always the opposite advice for any advice. The thing is, people are different. For some people they need to speak out more because they are quiet, while others speak too much and need to listen. You will only know who you are, which advice you came across and implicitly following, to see which of the two you need.

This applies a lot to doing a craft too. I make games. I like making particular games more than other games. Some advice will make you make one kind of game, and another advice will make you make another game. A common advice in games is:
> Show your game to players as fast as possible. Release early. Make it exist first, make it good later.

This advice floats around because it is useful to many people. It doesn't work for me at all. I'm already a very trigger happy person. When I release something, it feels like set in stone. I lose fluiditity. The advice I would give to my younger self is:
> Release only when you feel like it's time to release. Trust your instincts to keep it under wraps.

Of course, the "trust your instincts" part took a lot of work. I would've given this advice to myself only after I had already done quite a few years of releasing everything underbaked. Recently I've been hearing a lot from a friend who is getting into music making. She keeps saying "if I heard this advice 1 year ago I could've arrived at the same place in 6 months instead of a year!". I doubt it though. 1 year ago this advice wouldn't have made sense. Even if she followed it, it would've just led her in a different path, to different mistakes. And who knows, maybe eventually she would've done the same things she's been doing this past year because she had to learn those things by doing.

# Every advice is highly contextual
The hard part isn't finding advice. It's finding the advice you need. This is why it's very important to choose your mentors carefully. Pick the wrong one and they will make you skip the hard work and avoid mistakes you need to make. A good mentor will make you make mistakes quickly and with the least cost. 

I kinda like it really, how we surround ourselves with advice. What is a movie, a tv show, a game, but a collection of worldviews and advice? A story is a blueprint of context, conflict, and resolution. Entertainment is advice. "he's just like me fr" is you recognizing maybe this context matches your life situation. And maybe the way the author resolves it might apply to you...

If you get confused about an advice, ask yourself, what could have happened in the author's life for them to learn this lesson? Do you want to be more like them, or less?

# Advice is always the person talking to their younger self
It's hard to give advice. We look back and see all the wrong paths we went along. But those are critical. Those lets you know the land. Where are the thorny bushes? What's behind them? It only makes sense when you learn behind which thorns is the oasis and which is just more and more thorns. And maybe, in 10 years, after you developed thicker pants and a machete, you will find out that after more and more thorns there is the most beautiful waterfall you've ever seen. Or maybe if you just went around there was a paved path leading there.

"The best mentor you can get is your future self" is a thought I keep having. But I'm not sure. Every now and then a failed project from the past becomes the thing I just need today. I pull up a video from years ago so I can send to a friend, re-watch it, and find myself disagreeing with a good chunk of it. I find a video in my Watch Later and it tells me exactly what I need to hear now.

So, my advice for you: Don't be afraid to ignore advice. Make your own mistakes. And follow the fun. We build a world filled with advice. When you see no path forward, take a break. Watch something that looks interesting. Maybe it will tell you what you need to hear. 

Don't forget all the thorns that scraped you when you reach the oasis.


PS: We also have things that aren't advice. Tutorials, guides, methods. The difference is these solve a very specific problem. Do X, and Y **will happen**. Advice is about picking which method. A tutorial won't tell you if this is the solution *you* should go with.

There are also stories and bibliographies. Stories try harder to show you the context. The conflict. Bibliographies are often just about what a person did. Stories will give you clues on what the characters felt, so that you can see if you feel the same. In that sense bibliographies are more like tutorials. If you are the person A (in X situation) and do W then Q has happened in the past. So, it may just happen again if you, person B, in Y situation, do your own version of W, maybe Q will follow...
