I don't even have an excuse for waiting so long between these anymore. But here's the last of the reverse chronological ordered Games I've Played Recently! It's games I played 1 year ago at this point, but oh well. Part 4:

- **Tower Wizard:** Yet another short incremental game. In this one you are building a wizard tower. I don't really remember this one, but I had fun. It was fun enough to include in this list all the months ago I was collecting games for this anyways. Pretty standard incremental game, keeping the core while adding some fun twists probably.
- **World of Goo 2:** World of Goo is a game about making towers with little goo balls to reach a pipe in various levels as new stuff gets introduced like new goo types or environmental hazards. And this is the sequel for that game that released almost 20 years ago. This was an interesting one. I played the original World of Goo as a child, but I honestly wasn't too interested in even more World of Goo as I think the first one covered it well and the trailer for the 2nd one didn't have that many new things. But I was kinda confused about it winning awards. Then I watched a video about it. Minor spoilers, after you reach about 1/3 point, the game transforms into a critique/deconstruction of sequel culture and you play World of Goo 25 and 36 and whatnot. Then you even play a noir detective game. I liked the idea, but their execution felt half hearted. They do the critique of sequels, but in a lot of ways World of Goo 2 is also just a sequel with some side-games to break the main loop. Which is probably why it took me so long to play this one, even though I really like these sort of meta games (as a person who plays a lot of games lol). I wish they either leaned in all the way into the insane sequels idea, or did a total reconstruction like Stanley Parable's DLC does with DLCs. Oh well, still a fun enough game I guess.
- **Balatro:** In Balatro you draw cards, then pick some of them to make poker-like combos to earn points. But over the game you add more powerful cards to your deck, and get jokers like "all heart cards give you 2x multiplier". I waited very long to play this one because it just didn't appeal to me. But I figured I should play it just because its a big indie hit and I even saw people playing it while on an airplane soooo I gave it a try. And yep, it didn't appeal to me. I think its just a bit too random, too abstract? I also don't tend to like too much "gamefeel" heavy games. But if you like number go up but <img class="slack-emoji" src="/images/slack-sparkles-B960648DE033.png" alt=":sparkles:" /> Sparkly <img class="slack-emoji" src="/images/slack-sparkles-B960648DE033.png" alt=":sparkles:" />, with strategy mixed in, then give it a go!
- **Out of Hands:** In Out of Hands you are building a deck and fighting your childhood trauma as a depressed(?) guy. Oh and everything in the game is made out of hands, very cool artstyle. I really wanted to like this game, because the topic is interesting, the art is interesting, I like deckbuilders. But its just a bit too in your face with the story? Like its both abstract, like you are in a school and a particular classroom is dark and you need some special item to get there, and you think "oh I wonder what happened there!". Then the game goes "oh btw the X bad event happened in that classroom so I need the -X solver item that does Y- to enter there to fix my trauma" which just made me feel stupid trying to figure out the meaning behind things, and also sometimes I was wrong which never feels nice lol. Soo I dropped this one halfway-ish through. Also the combat becomes too repetitive some hours in. But still nice to play just for the artstyle and a couple interesting things they did for the deckbuilding.
- **20 Minutes Till Dawn:** In this roguelike Vampire Survivors-like you are trying to survive 20 minutes while monsters attack you from all sides and you collect xp and get upgrades. I wanted to play this one because it made the indie news for a bit because this one guy played Vampire Survivors (a surprise viral indie hit) and made a similar but different game in like 2 months and captured a good chunk of the popularity (which arguably led to some of the friendslop follow up games that came after the likes of Lethal Company because it was proven to be a good strategy). And 20 Minutes Till Dawn is a nice game indeed. But it's also a bit too streamlined, too clean to me. Oh well.

And that's about it. This one ended up being extra big too because I wanted to get all of the remaining ones out in one go. But my debt is paid. I can return to actually recent games I've played once again. I already played a bunch of fun and interesting games I want to share, so wait for those!
